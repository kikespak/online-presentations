Context

Where we are

Until now the account has been fed post by post, each one decided separately. That produces good individual pieces but no momentum.

What I have proposed is brand design applied to Instagram: a recognisable visual signature, built from material you already own. Your own words, in your own hand, on a considered image, arriving on a rhythm.

It comes from you rather than from a template, and it puts your writing in front of people rather than talking about your writing.

The plan below covers three months: what gets published, what gets advertised, what it costs, and what we look at to know it is working.

Objectives

It is worth saying plainly what the advertising is aiming at, so that the effort goes behind an agreed goal rather than a vague one.

01
To build an audience that recognises you

People who see a post and know it is yours before they read the name.

To turn that audience into readers

Content

The rhythm

One to two posts a week, made in a monthly batch and scheduled in advance. Everything for the month is prepared, you approve it in one sitting, and then it runs without either of us thinking about it again. The format varies inside that rhythm, so the Instagram account stays alive.

The quote card
Your handwriting on a chosen image.
The recognisable core. This is the signature.
The quote in motion
The same quote as a short clip of six to ten seconds: the ink appearing, a slow move across the paper, or the written note held in the foreground.
Instagram shows short video to far more people than it shows still images, and it costs no extra to make.
The hand at work
Close-up of the line being written.
Intimate, and no need to be on camera.
Your voice
You reading one line aloud, occasionally.
Hearing a writer read their own line is what turns a stranger into a reader.
Stories
Resharing each post, a question, a countdown before an event.
Free, quick, and where the people who already follow you actually look.
The number of posts stays at one to two a week. The variety happens inside that number.

Advertising

Advertising, in three steps

The role of advertising here is to act as a booster rather than a switch. It takes work that has already proved itself and puts it in front of more of the right people. It is closer to a scientific process than to a purchase, and it needs its own timing: look at what happens, form a theory, run a small experiment, refine the theory, then run a better one.

STEP 01 · WEEKS 1–2
Setting up

There is no advertising account yet, so one is created in your name, with your card on it. I also add a small piece of code to both websites, which simply tells us which visits and which sales came from Instagram. No advertising money is spent in this step.

STEP 02 · WEEKS 2–8
Building the audience

The quote posts run. Some do noticeably better than others, and we know which rather than guessing. Those are the ones we put money behind, aimed at bringing people to your profile. Using posts that have already proved themselves is the single biggest difference between advertising that works and advertising that does not.

STEP 03 · FROM WEEK 6
Books and events

Now selling makes sense, because we are speaking to people who have already met your words. This step also draws on something built quietly in step two: a record of everyone who watched, reacted or visited the site, who can be reached again for a fraction of the cost.
